---
title: "Estate Planning for Young Families"
description: "Why estate planning matters earlier than most young families assume, and what the basics actually involve."
---

Estate planning feels like a concern for later in life, but a few basics matter significantly earlier for anyone with dependents.

## A Will Matters More With Children Involved
Beyond asset distribution, a will allows parents to formally name a guardian for minor children — arguably its most important function for young families.

## Beneficiary Designations Need Regular Review
Retirement accounts and life insurance pass directly to named beneficiaries regardless of what a will says, making outdated beneficiary designations a common and costly oversight.

## Basic Documents Beyond a Will
A healthcare directive and power of attorney address decisions if you're incapacitated, not just after death — often overlooked in basic estate planning.

## This Doesn't Require Significant Wealth
Estate planning is frequently misunderstood as only relevant for wealthy families — the guardian-naming and incapacity planning functions matter at any income level.

## The Bottom Line
Basic estate planning, especially guardian designation, is relevant for any family with minor children regardless of asset level — not just a concern for later in life.
