---
title: "Roth IRA vs Traditional IRA: Which Retirement Account Wins"
description: "A breakdown of the key differences between Roth and Traditional IRAs to help decide which fits your situation."
---

This isn't a question of which account is objectively better — it depends on when you expect to pay less tax: now or in retirement.

## The Core Difference
Traditional IRA contributions are typically tax-deductible now, with withdrawals taxed in retirement. Roth IRA contributions are made with after-tax money, with withdrawals tax-free in retirement.

## Why Current vs. Future Tax Bracket Matters
If you expect to be in a lower tax bracket in retirement than now, Traditional often wins. If you expect a similar or higher bracket later, Roth often comes out ahead.

## Roth's Flexibility Advantage
Roth IRAs allow original contributions (not earnings) to be withdrawn without penalty in many cases, offering more flexibility than Traditional accounts if plans change.

## Income Limits Can Affect Eligibility
Roth IRA contributions phase out at higher income levels, which can make the decision for some higher earners rather than leaving it purely to preference.

## The Bottom Line
There's no universally "better" option — the right choice depends on your current tax situation and genuine best guess about your future one.
