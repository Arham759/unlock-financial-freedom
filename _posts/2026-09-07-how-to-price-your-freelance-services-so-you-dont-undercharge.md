---
title: "How to Price Your Freelance Services So You Don't Undercharge"
description: "A practical framework for pricing freelance work without leaving money on the table."
---

Underpricing is one of the most common and costly mistakes new freelancers make — usually out of fear rather than bad math.

## Hourly Pricing Punishes Efficiency
Charging hourly means getting faster at your work directly lowers your income. Project-based or value-based pricing rewards skill and speed instead of penalizing it.

## Research Market Rates Before Guessing
Checking what others with similar experience charge for comparable work — rather than picking an arbitrary low number — prevents the common trap of pricing based on self-doubt.

## Factor In Non-Billable Time
Client communication, revisions, and admin work all take real time that isn't directly billable — pricing that ignores this consistently underpays for the actual work involved.

## Raise Prices With Each New Client, Not All at Once
Gradually increasing rates for new clients (while honoring existing agreements) avoids the shock of a sudden jump while still correcting underpricing over time.

## The Bottom Line
Confident, research-based pricing protects your time and signals professionalism — underpricing rarely wins better clients, it just attracts more demanding ones.
