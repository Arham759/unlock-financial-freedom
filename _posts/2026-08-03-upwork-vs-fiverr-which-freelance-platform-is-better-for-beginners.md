Both platforms get recommended constantly, but they work in opposite ways — and picking the wrong one for your situation wastes months.

## How Each Platform Works
Fiverr is buyer-initiated: you list fixed-price "gigs" and wait for orders. Upwork is proposal-based: you browse job posts and pitch yourself, often competing against dozens of other freelancers per listing.

## Where Beginners Struggle on Each
On Upwork, new accounts face a visibility problem — clients filter by "job success score," which you don't have yet. On Fiverr, the problem is discovery — your gig is buried unless it's optimized and lucky early on.

## Pricing Differences
Fiverr skews toward smaller, fixed-price tasks. Upwork supports hourly contracts and larger ongoing projects, which suits service-based freelancers (like VAs or consultants) better than one-off task sellers.

## Which to Pick First
If your service is a clearly packaged deliverable (a template, a short video edit, a logo), start with Fiverr. If you're offering ongoing work (bookkeeping, writing, admin support), Upwork's proposal model fits better.

## The Bottom Line
Neither platform is "better" universally — match the platform's model to how your service is actually sold.
