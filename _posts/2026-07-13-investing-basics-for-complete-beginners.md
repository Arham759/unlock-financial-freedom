Investing terminology creates an unnecessary barrier to getting started — the core concepts are simpler than the jargon suggests.

## Stocks Represent Ownership
Buying a stock means owning a small piece of a company, with returns coming from price appreciation and sometimes dividends.

## Funds Spread Risk Automatically
Index funds and ETFs bundle many stocks together, reducing the risk of any single company's poor performance significantly affecting your investment.

## Risk and Time Horizon Are Connected
Generally, a longer time horizon before needing the money allows for more risk tolerance, since there's more time to recover from downturns.

## Starting Small Still Counts as Starting
Waiting to "have enough" to invest meaningfully delays the real advantage of investing, which is time in the market.

## The Bottom Line
Understanding these core concepts — ownership, diversification, time horizon — matters more at the start than picking the "perfect" first investment.
