Dividend investing gets marketed as "passive income," but understanding how the payments actually work prevents unrealistic expectations.

## What a Dividend Actually Is
A dividend is a portion of a company's profit paid out to shareholders, usually quarterly. Not all companies pay dividends — many reinvest profits into growth instead.

## Dividend Yield Isn't the Whole Picture
A high dividend yield can sometimes signal a struggling stock price rather than a generous company — yield is a ratio, and a falling stock price raises it even if the dividend itself hasn't changed.

## Reinvesting vs. Taking the Cash
Automatically reinvesting dividends to buy more shares compounds growth over time, while taking the cash provides more immediate usable income — the right choice depends on your goals and timeline.

## This Is Not Fast Passive Income
Meaningful dividend income requires a substantial invested amount — this is a long-term wealth-building strategy, not a quick side income source.

## The Bottom Line
Dividend investing rewards patience and reinvestment far more than chasing the highest yield number you can find.
