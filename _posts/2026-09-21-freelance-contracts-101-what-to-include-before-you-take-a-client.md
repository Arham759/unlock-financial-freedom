---
title: "Freelance Contracts 101: What to Include Before You Take a Client"
description: "The essential elements every freelance contract should include to protect both the freelancer and the client."
---

Skipping a contract to "keep things simple" is one of the most common and costly mistakes new freelancers make.

## Scope of Work, Specifically
Vaguely defined deliverables are the single biggest source of freelance disputes — a contract should clearly state exactly what is and isn't included.

## Payment Terms and Timeline
Clear payment amounts, due dates, and what happens if payment is late protect you from the common pattern of chasing overdue invoices with no agreed recourse.

## Revision Limits
Without a stated limit on revision rounds, scope can quietly expand indefinitely — specifying a reasonable number of revisions protects your time.

## Kill Clause / Early Termination Terms
Defining what happens if either party wants to end the engagement early — including partial payment for work already completed — avoids messy, unresolved situations.

## The Bottom Line
A simple, clear contract protects the relationship as much as it protects you — most clients respect clear expectations rather than resenting them.
