---

Micro-investing apps make starting easy, but their fee structures can quietly undercut small account balances if you're not paying attention.

## How Micro-Investing Apps Work
These apps let you invest very small amounts, often rounding up purchases or allowing contributions as low as a few dollars, usually into diversified fund portfolios.

## The Fee Problem at Small Balances
A flat monthly fee that seems small in dollar terms can represent a large percentage of a very small account balance, quietly eating returns disproportionately.

## Where They Genuinely Help
For people who would otherwise not invest at all due to the psychological barrier of "not having enough," these apps lower that barrier meaningfully and build a useful habit.

## When to Graduate to a Standard Brokerage
Once your balance grows enough that a flat fee becomes a small percentage, or once you're comfortable with basic investing concepts, a standard low-fee brokerage often becomes more cost-effective.

## The Bottom Line
Micro-investing apps are a reasonable on-ramp for building the habit, but worth reassessing as your balance and knowledge grow.
