Fiverr looks simple until you publish your first gig and hear nothing for two weeks. The platform rewards specificity, not generalism — here's what actually moves the needle.

## Pick a Service, Not a Skill
"Graphic design" is a skill. "Instagram carousel templates for fitness coaches" is a service. Buyers search for solutions to specific problems, not broad categories. Narrow your gig title until it describes exactly who it's for.

## Price Low on Gig One, Not Forever
Your first few orders exist to generate reviews, not profit. Price your entry package low enough to get 5-10 completed orders fast, then raise prices as your review count and response rate build trust.

## Your Gig Thumbnail Is Doing More Work Than Your Description
Most buyers scroll thumbnails before reading anything. Invest in a clean, specific thumbnail image that shows the actual output, not a generic stock graphic.

## Respond Within the Hour
Fiverr's algorithm favors sellers with fast response times. Turning on notifications and replying quickly in your first month matters more than almost anything else for gig visibility.

## The Bottom Line
Fiverr isn't passive — it rewards narrow positioning and fast communication over broad skill and low prices. Start specific, deliver well, and let reviews do the selling for you.
