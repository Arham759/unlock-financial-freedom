Generic housing affordability rules offer a starting point, but a fuller calculation accounts for more than just a percentage of income.

## The 28% Guideline and Its Limits
A commonly cited guideline suggests housing costs shouldn't exceed roughly 28% of gross income, though this doesn't account for other debt obligations.

## Total Debt-to-Income Matters More
Factoring in all debt obligations, not just housing, gives a more complete picture of actual affordability than the housing percentage alone.

## Don't Forget True Total Housing Cost
Property taxes, insurance, and maintenance add meaningfully to a mortgage payment alone — affordability calculations that ignore these understate true cost.

## Regional Cost of Living Shifts Everything
A fixed percentage rule means something very different in a low-cost versus high-cost region — local context matters more than the universal number.

## The Bottom Line
A complete affordability calculation accounts for total debt, full housing costs beyond the mortgage, and regional context — not just a single income percentage.
