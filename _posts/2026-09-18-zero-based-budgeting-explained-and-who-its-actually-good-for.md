Zero-based budgeting has a reputation for being intense — understanding what it actually requires helps decide if that intensity is worth it for your situation.

## The Core Idea
Every dollar of income is assigned a specific job — spending, saving, or debt payoff — until income minus allocations equals zero. Nothing is left unaccounted for.

## Why It Works Well for Irregular Income
Because it's rebuilt each period based on actual income rather than a fixed assumed amount, it adapts naturally to freelance or variable paychecks better than fixed-category budgets.

## The Real Time Cost
Zero-based budgeting requires more regular maintenance than simpler methods — it rewards people willing to revisit their budget monthly, not those looking for a "set and forget" system.

## Who Tends to Struggle With It
People who find detailed tracking draining rather than satisfying often abandon zero-based budgeting within a few months — a simpler percentage-based method may stick better long-term.

## The Bottom Line
Zero-based budgeting is powerful for people who want full visibility and don't mind the maintenance — not a universal best option.
