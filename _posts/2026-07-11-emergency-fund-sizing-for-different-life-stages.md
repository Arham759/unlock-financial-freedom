---
title: "Emergency Fund Sizing for Different Life Stages"
description: "How emergency fund targets should realistically shift across different life stages."
---

A single universal emergency fund target doesn't account for how risk and obligations shift across life stages.

## Early Career, Single
Lower fixed obligations generally mean a smaller fund (around 3 months of expenses) provides reasonable protection at this stage.

## Family With Dependents
More fixed obligations and dependents relying on your income generally justify a larger fund, often closer to 6 months of expenses.

## Single-Income Households
Households relying on one income source carry more risk from job loss, generally justifying a larger buffer than dual-income households.

## Near Retirement
Reduced ability to quickly replace lost income through new employment often justifies a larger buffer even with stable current income.

## The Bottom Line
Emergency fund size should scale with financial obligations and income replaceability, not stay fixed at a single number across your whole life.
