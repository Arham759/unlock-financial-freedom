Budgeting fails most often not from bad math, but from systems too complicated to maintain consistently.

## Start With Tracking, Not Restricting
Before setting spending limits, simply tracking where money currently goes for a month reveals patterns most people don't expect.

## Choose a Method That Matches Your Personality
Detailed trackers suit people who enjoy data; simpler percentage-based methods suit people who find detailed tracking draining — the "best" method is the one you'll actually maintain.

## Review Monthly, Not Daily
Checking in on your budget monthly rather than obsessively daily reduces burnout while still catching problems early enough to adjust.

## Build In Flexibility From the Start
A budget with zero room for discretionary spending tends to get abandoned — building in some flexibility improves long-term adherence.

## The Bottom Line
The most effective budget is the simplest one you'll actually stick with consistently, not the most detailed one possible.
